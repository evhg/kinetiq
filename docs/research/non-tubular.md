# Non-tubular construction: terms and positioning

Research run 2026-10-21 (web sources in English and Spanish, patents, engineering references), reviewed by a
skeptical composites and advertising-standards pass. Everything that applies a term to KINETIQ is conditional
on the factory confirming the construction (questions at the end). The factory is not named in public files.

## Answer in one line

KINETIQ is not a "closed bridge", "hook solid" or "ultra flat" design. The accurate description is a
**one-piece carbon shell with no frame tube** (engineering: a stressed-skin monocoque, made by closed-mould
resin transfer moulding over a melt-out core), with **moulded, not drilled, holes**. On the site:
"No tube. The shell is the frame."

## Terms

| Term | Meaning | Where to use it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No frame tube | There is no separate hollow tube around the head: the carbon faces close around the edge and become the frame themselves. | The main buyer term and proof line (hero, chapter, reserve page). Body copy always says 'frame tube'. Never use 'tubeless' or 'tube-free' as a literal claim: the handle, the 42 hole walls and any hollow rim are closed sections, and another brand already lists 'tubeless frame' next to 'twin-tube'. |
| One carbon shell / The shell is the frame | Face, edge, bridge and handle are one moulded carbon skin that carries every load, so the skin is the structure. | Consumer name for the architecture and headline language. Later, give it a coined name with the brand in it (for example 'KINETIQ Shell') after a trademark search, so the difference has a name nobody else owns. |
| Wrapped-edge sandwich (if foam-cored) / single-cell closed shell (if hollow) | Two carbon skins that wrap the rim and close around a foam or hollow interior, instead of ending against a separate edge beam. | Specs page, FAQ and press kit. Pick the variant once the factory says which regions are foam-filled and which are hollow. |
| Monocoque | A structure whose outer skin carries the load, with no separate internal frame. | Specs table only, as a description ('one-piece carbon monocoque'). Never as a headline, and never with 'first' or 'only': at least three other padel brands or filings already use the word. |
| Closed-mould RTM (resin transfer moulding) | Dry carbon fabric is placed in a closed, machined aluminium mould and resin is injected into it, unlike the prepreg-and-bladder press used to blow tube frames. | Specs and press. This is the most precise, checkable process difference from tubular rackets. |
| Lost-core moulding (melt-out wax core) | The carbon is laid over a wax pattern that is melted out after curing, leaving the shell's interior free. | Specs and FAQ, worded as 'moulded over a wax core that is then melted out'. Never 'lost-wax cast', which is a metal-casting process. |
| One-shot moulding | Every fibre, insert and core is in the mould for a single resin injection and a single cure, and nothing structural is bonded on afterwards. | Only after the factory confirms it in writing. Until then, say 'one-piece moulded shell'. |
| Perimeter weighting | Mass placed at the 3 and 9 o'clock edges of the face, which raises twistweight so the racket resists twisting on off-centre hits. | The real mechanism behind the sweet-spot story. Buyer wording: 'weight moulded in at the sides'. It is a different thing from the balance weights on the long axis (CLAIMS row 7), which do not widen the sweet spot. |
| Sweet spot (high-rebound zone) | The area of the face where ball rebound stays within 90% of the racket's own best point, measured on a rebound (ACOR) map. | State this definition on the specs page with the lab result. Never claim 'bigger' or 'wider' without naming the definition and the test. |
| Moulded holes | Holes formed by pins in the mould so the fibres flow around each one, instead of being cut by a drill after curing. | Keep it as the strongest checkable proof point, next to the shell story (spec strip, holes section). |

## The owner's candidate terms

| Term | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| Non-tubular | Right idea, wrong headline. It defines KINETIQ by what it lacks. It is also open to attack: a hollow rim, the handle and the hole walls are all closed sections. Use the positive form 'No frame tube. The shell is the frame.' and keep 'non-tubular' out of the copy. |
| Tubeless / tube-free | Avoid as a literal claim, for the same reasons. Another brand already sells 'tubeless frame' rackets that also advertise a twin-tube system, so the word is blurred and gives no ownership. |
| Closed bridge | A real padel term, but for throat shape, not construction, and many tubular rackets have one. Use it only as a spec, and only if the throat has no opening. Better: 'the bridge is part of the same shell'. This may be where a genuine torsion gain sits; measure it before claiming 'stiffer in twist'. |
| Hook solid | Avoid entirely. 'Solid' is a model name of HOOK Padel, 'hook' or 'hookless' is bicycle-rim vocabulary, and 'solid' misdescribes a hollow or foam-filled shell. |
| Ultra-flat carbon layouts | Avoid. The FIP rules already require flat faces, so 'flat' sounds like a rule requirement. The technical term for very flat, thin-tow fabric is spread-tow carbon (TeXtreme), which is a face material, not a frame design, and other padel brands already use it. Mention 'spread-tow' in the specs only if the factory uses it. If the point is mould flatness, claim a measured surface value instead. |
| Increased sweet spot | Plausible, but tube-free does not give it on its own. Taking the tube out removes weight from the perimeter, which by itself narrows the sweet spot. It widens only if that weight goes back in at 3 and 9 o'clock. Under the owner's 'claim boldly now, verify before launch' rule it can appear in the preview as 'the grams a tube spends on structure go to the sides, where they widen the sweet spot'. Before launch, an independent rebound (ACOR) map against tubular rackets is required. Fallback if it fails: a measured consistency claim. |
| Monocoque | Accurate but not ownable. DOPE Rackets, Babolat x Lamborghini and BAS3LINE already use it, and BAS3LINE's version still has a double tube. Use it in the specs table only. |
| World's first one-piece / 'Every padel racket is glued. Except one.' | Retire both. DOPE Rackets publishes 'carbon monocoque ... single piece'. Tubular rackets are cured together in one press, not glued in the adhesive sense. A claim about all competitors' goods invites an unfair-competition complaint. Make the contrast about the tube, not about glue. |
| The factory's own vocabulary | Its patent uses "integrally formed in one piece", "removable pattern", "hollow device" and resin transfer moulding. None of the four candidate terms appear in it. |

## Contrast lines (for later chapters and ads)

- Most padel rackets are built around a tube. KINETIQ is one shell.
- Elsewhere, the faces sit on a frame. Here, the faces are the frame.
- A tube spends its grams holding a racket together. We spend ours on the sweet spot.
- No tube to build around, so every gram goes where we design it.
- Most rackets meet a drill after the mould. Ours never do.
- Built around nothing. Built as one.

## Questions for the factory

1. Is the finished racket hollow, foam-injected after the wax is melted out, or built on a retained foam core? Which regions are hollow and which are foam-filled? This decides 'wrapped-edge sandwich' versus 'single-cell closed shell'.
2. Are the head, bridge and handle made in one resin injection and one cure? Is anything bonded on afterwards (handle insert, end cap, cord anchor, edge guard)?
3. Do the face plies wrap around the edge into the other face? Where are the overlaps and splices? Please share the ply book.
4. How does the wax drain? Is any opening machined after curing, or does it leave through a moulded hole or the handle end?
5. Which inserts are moulded in, where do they sit and what do they weigh? Can weight be placed at 3 and 9 o'clock, and how much?
6. Is the bridge closed and moulded into the handle as one closed section?
7. Are all 42 holes formed by mould pins, with zero drilling, reaming or trimming?
8. How thick is the rim edge compared with a typical tube rail? How much more active face does that leave inside the FIP size limits?
9. Fibre: woven 3K or 12K, UD or spread-tow?
10. Is the racket process covered by the granted JP and AU patents and the pending European and US applications? Does KINETIQ have a licence, and what exact wording is permitted?
11. Will you supply 3 production rackets for CT scans and an independent rebound-map and twistweight test against tubular rackets?
12. Do you know of prior art: DOPE Rackets' single-piece monocoque, Babolat x Lamborghini's peripheral monocoque, or any padel racket with moulded holes?
